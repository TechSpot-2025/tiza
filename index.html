Three steps. You only do the first.

  1. Add your class

    Paste the Zoom, Teams or Meet link and pick the days and time. Or connect Canvas and pick the class from your course’s Zoom tab; Tiza fills in the rest.

    Your class is on

  2. Tiza opens the class and records it

    By default, two minutes early it opens the class window and records video and audio. Camera off. Mic off unless you turn it on. If the class needs a click (a waiting room, a passcode), you do that once.

  3. The file lands in your cloud

    When class ends, the MP4 uploads to the cloud you picked. A copy stays on your computer too, unless you turn that off.

Your class, your cloud. Not ours.

  1. Class window
  2. Your computer MP4
  3. Your cloud
  4. Tiza server: there isn’t one
  • No Tiza account. Nothing to sign up for.
  • No extra attendee. Tiza joins under your name, from your computer: in your own account, or as a guest if the platform blocks signing in.
  • Tiza never sees your Zoom, Teams or Google password. You sign in once, on their own page, inside the app.
  • Cloud access is approved on Google’s, Microsoft’s or Dropbox’s own page in your browser. The permission token is stored encrypted on your computer and you can revoke it from your account anytime.
  • No AI reads your class. Tiza records; it doesn’t transcribe or summarize.
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no cookies. This page loads only its fonts from Fontshare and Google Fonts.

What Tiza doesn’t do

  • Send a bot to your class
  • Transcribe or summarize
  • Keep your platform passwords
  • Record while your computer is off
  • Run on a phone
  • Turn your camera on

Before recording, check the rules.

Some classes allow personal recordings. Some don’t. Check your syllabus, then your school’s policy, and if you’re not sure, ask your instructor. Recording without permission can break school rules and, depending on where you and your classmates are, recording laws.

Tiza joins under your name, with the camera off and the mic off by default, so the class may show you as present and may not show a recording notice. If your class takes attendance or participation, that’s on you. Keep recordings for your own study; don’t post or share them. Have a recording accommodation? Follow its terms.

Hi Professor ___, I’d like to record our online sessions for my own review. I’d keep the recordings private and wouldn’t share them. Is that OK with you? Thanks, ___

This isn’t legal advice.

First time opening it

The beta builds aren’t signed yet, so your computer will warn you the first time.

  • Mac: if macOS says it can’t verify the developer, open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway, then confirm.
  • Windows: if you see “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway. On a new Windows 11 PC with Smart App Control turned on, an unsigned app can be blocked outright; that PC needs a signed build.

On a Mac, the first run asks for permission to record the screen and audio. That’s how it records the class window.

Before you rely on it, run Settings → Test recording inside the app and watch the 8-second file.

Questions you’d ask a friend

Is it OK to record my class?
It depends on your class and your school. Check your syllabus and your school’s policy, and if you’re not sure, ask your instructor. Some schools require permission even for personal use. Keep recordings for your own study; don’t post or share them. The rules card above has a message you can send.
Will my professor see me in the class?
Yes. Tiza opens the class under your name (your own account, or as a guest with your name if Google Meet blocks signing in), with the camera off and the mic off by default. If your class takes attendance or calls on people, you’re responsible for that.
Where does the recording go, and who can see it?
An MP4 goes to the cloud you pick: Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, or your iCloud Drive folder, which your computer syncs. It lands in your account, so only you and anyone you share it with can see it. There’s no Tiza server in between. A copy also stays on your computer unless you turn that off. Free cloud plans fill up fast, so check your space.
Does Tiza need my Zoom, Teams or Google password?
No. You sign in once inside the app, on Zoom’s, Microsoft’s or Google’s own page, and the app remembers that session. Tiza never stores your password. For your cloud, you approve access on the provider’s own page in your browser, and the permission token is kept encrypted on your computer.
Does my computer need to be on and awake?
It needs to be on, with Tiza running. Tiza can open when you log in and wait in your menu bar or tray. If your computer might be asleep, turn on “Wake before class” and Tiza wakes it a few minutes early; on a Mac that asks for your admin password whenever it schedules the next wake-ups, so you’ll see the prompt now and then. If the computer is off or out of battery, Tiza can’t record, so keep it plugged in.
What if it can’t get into the class?
Joining is automatic when it can be. A passcode, a waiting room or an expired sign-in can need you once; the class window stays open so you can click. If the link changes, open the class in Tiza and paste the new one. Classes added from Canvas keep the course’s lasting Zoom link, not Canvas’s one-time link. Run the test recording in Settings before the first real class.
Why not just press Record in Zoom?
Often only the host can record, and the host’s cloud recordings live in their account; some schools delete them after a few weeks. Tiza records on your side, starts on its own at class time, and keeps the file in your cloud. If your school already posts every recording, you may not need Tiza at all.
Is it free? Mac, Windows, phone? Is the app in English?
Free during the beta; a Pro plan may come later, with no price decided. macOS 13 or later and Windows 10 or 11. There’s no phone app: recording a class needs a computer. The app’s own screens are in Spanish today; this page is in both languages.

Be in class when you can. Have the recording when you can’t.
